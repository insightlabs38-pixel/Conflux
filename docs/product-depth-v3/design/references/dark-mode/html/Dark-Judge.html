Summary. Northwind forecasts substation-level load 1–24 h ahead using a weather-conditioned sequence model, cutting 24 h MAE by 21.5 % against NWP-Lite on 412 substations while running in 38 ms on a single CPU core.

Method

For substation i and horizon h we minimise a pinball-smoothed absolute error and evaluate with MAEh = (1/N) Σ |ŷi,h − yi,h| (Eq. 1). Weather embeddings are computed once per cell and shared across substations, which is what makes 412 substations fit in 38 ms on one core.

1def mae(pred, y, h):2    err = np.abs(pred[:, h] - y[:, h])3    return float(err.mean())

Evaluation

Numbers are 5-seed means with 95 % bootstrap intervals over held-out substations. Removing the weather-embedding branch raises 24 h MAE by 11.4 %; removing calibration raises it by 5.4 %. Gains are largest on substations with under 18 months of history, where NWP-Lite degrades fastest.

Limitations

Performance drops on substations with under 18 months of history; extreme weather regimes are under-represented in training.
